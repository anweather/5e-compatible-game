# Retrospective: 25–28 Oct 2026

## The project
A Unity 6 multiplayer sandbox, compatible with D&D 5e. Wizards cast spells into a lockstep chemistry simulation: fire, water, earth, cold, lightning and weather. It plays out over a streamed valley with villages, a coast and mini games (tower defense, the ball court, the climbing tower). Alpha builds ship to Windows and macOS through Velopack. From 28 Oct the focus is how materials, their properties and effects interact, spell effects, and the mini games. The look and feel of the world is a side quest.

## How we work
One coordinator session writes no game code. It sets lanes, owns COORDINATION.md and the judgment-call log, reviews and orders merges into `dev`, and reports to Andrew. Three agent sessions own their lanes, with one owner per file: Gameplay (PC), World (the chemistry core, rules, water and the playtest bot; PC) and Build (performance and releases; Mac). Every merge needs checks: tests, smoke, probes with numbers, and a before/after shot. Releases are split, the PC building Windows and the Mac building macOS into one draft. The playtest bot runs on release candidates. On 28 Oct we switched to frugal serial mode: one agent at a time, small goals, and merges pre-approved when their checks pass.

## Wins
- **Shipping speed.** Alpha 0.1.4 to 0.1.6 shipped in about three days, with about 70 merges, and most of Andrew's 25+ playtest paper cuts were fixed within a day of being reported.
- **Lanes and the judgment-call log.** Owning files kept merge conflicts rare. 36 logged calls kept decisions consistent across sessions without asking Andrew twice.
- **The serial materials push (28 Oct).** Eight small merges, each checked with probes, built the start of the materials system: soaking in, three new ground types, materials for objects and buildings, walls and roofs failing separately, burn rates, fire looks and a design note. None of them broke anything.

## Misses
- **The PC was overloaded.** Two or three Editors on 31 GB, windows stealing focus, and a script waiting forever for an Editor that never opened all cost hours until the J-017 rules and the idle alert went in.
- **Tokens burned fast.** Running sessions in parallel, the coordinator relaying everything, the demo reel and the skills work ran usage down. That forced frugal mode.
- **Drift and slips.** Effort went into look and feel (three rounds of cloth, the sky, Punchy research) more than the materials and mini games. There were also process slips: `--force` past the preflight, merges without a go, and edits in other lanes.
