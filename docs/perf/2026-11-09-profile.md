# The 60 fps profile, step 1 (2026-11-09)

The target is 16.7 ms a frame for the mean and the p95 (STATE.md "Next" item 1). This is the measuring
step, with no fixes yet. It was measured at dev a49e195d (72482293 plus a queue note) on the Windows PC, in
the open Editor (`.claude/worktrees/host-ux`) and in a development player built from the same tree
(`unity/Builds/PerfProfile/`, `PrototypeBuild.Build(..., BuildOptions.Development)`, not tagged or uploaded).

## The answer first

- **The valley is under 16.7 ms in a player, but its p95 is not on a walk.** A fixed camera runs at 8.2 / 13.6 ms
  and the playtest bot's walk at 11.9 / 20.8 ms.
- **The nine is over 16.7 ms in a player even with a fixed camera**, at 19.5-20.6 / 26.3-27.6 ms. The bot's walk
  on the nine runs at 31.6 / 85.0 ms, because of 360-410 ms hitches in `ChemGrid.RenderGas` once a Fireball's
  fire has smoke.
- **The nine costs about 11 ms a frame more than the valley under the same camera.** Most of that is
  `ChemGrid.ChangeGround` running every frame (3 ms), PhysX broadphase (3 ms), `ChemGrid.Render`'s other work
  (+2.5 ms) and rendering (+1.8 ms).
- **farringclip's worst frame (186-278 ms) is the probe's own work**: `FarRingClipProbe.Stop()` (154-189 ms
  self) ends every run. In the same runs, a real ~150 ms `ChemGrid.Step` frame sits two frames after the
  probe's rule switch (`Switch`): `World.ApplyRules` on the nine, with 4.7 MB of garbage.
- **far-looks' p95 "regression" is not real, or at least can't be told apart from noise.** Four runs at the same
  commit gave p95 42.8, 46.4, 87.7 and 65.2 ms. The p95 follows the filming's garbage (1.4 MB a frame, a
  50-100 ms GC every ~100 frames) and whatever else the PC is doing.

## The two leads

**farringclip's worst frame.** In every run the worst frame is the probe's last: `FarRingClipProbe.Stop()`
(154 ms self in run 2, 189 in run 3) as the film is written out. That frame falls inside the measured window.
The next worst in each run is real game work: `ChemGrid.Step` at 150-154 ms with 4.7 MB of garbage, two frames
after `FarRingClipProbe.Switch()` sets the hour and weather. A rule switch is applied inside the step
(`StepWithinBudget` → `ApplyQueuedRules` → `World.ApplyRules`, under the `ChemGrid.Step` marker, as the core has
no markers of its own). The player shows the same frame after `PerfStage`'s own switch: frame 9, `ChemGrid.Step`
137 ms, and frame 65, 126 ms with 4.9 MB allocated, both before measuring starts. The rest are the film's GC
collects (50-104 ms, every ~100 frames). So 186 ms is either the film's stop or the rule switch on the nine,
and neither is a frame that normal play produces regularly. The rule switch is real for anyone who toggles a
mod or a rule on the nine.

**far-looks' p95 (51 ms, then 40.9).** Four runs today at one commit: 42.8, 46.4, 87.7, 65.2 ms. Runs 3 and 4 were
slower everywhere, including `Idle`, `MasterDSP` and the Profiler's own flush (frames 1700-1960 of run 3), which
means the machine was busy, not the game. The filming adds 1.4 MB of garbage a frame and a 50-100 ms collect
every ~100 frames. At ~950 frames a run, that's about 10 of the ~48 frames above the p95, so the p95 sits on the
GC's timing and the PC's load. farringclip is a filming probe and isn't fit to judge a p95 change of ±10 ms. Use
a player `-perf-scenario` run for that, or a probe that doesn't film.

## Fix 1 (#179, #180): the nine draws only what its haze shows

**The cause was one thing, not two.** Nothing on the nine redresses twice. A count of every `ChangeGround` call over a
20 s Editor run on the nine gave 1.9 million calls, each cell once, from no terrain to its own (`null -> grass`, `null ->
tree`, ...). No river cells were involved. `ChangeGround`'s 3.1 ms was the first draw of the map's chunks. After a build
`DrawSome` draws every chunk a few a frame, nearest first, for its 3 ms budget. The valley's 81 chunks are done before
measuring starts, but the nine's 729 took about a minute. Every blocking cell drawn (54 000 trees, 44 000 brush, stone
and the rest) took a new `BoxCollider` from `CellBlockers`, so PhysX inserted static colliders every frame. That was the
broadphase's 3.2 ms. The blockers are already pooled, but a first draw has nothing in the pool to reuse. A 120 s run
of the before build showed it: at 45-50 s into measuring the draw ended, and the frame fell from 22.5 to 13.2 ms,
`ChangeGround` from 3.1 to 0 and `Physics.Simulate` from 4.4 to 0.65.

**The fix** (`ChemGrid.Visuals.cs`, `QueueToDraw` / `DrawRadius`): on a generated map, `DrawSome` queues only the
undrawn chunks within the far ring's known radius (`FarRing.Dials.known`, 7 chunks) of a focus. It queues them again
when a focus moves a chunk or the radius changes, so a walk draws ahead of itself. Past that radius the haze is ~98% and
the land is a known mesh, so nothing seen changes. The valley has no far ring and still draws every chunk.
`ChunksLeftToDraw` and the hiding of the old world's spare blockers count only the queued chunks. View only (D4).

**Nothing drawn differently.** In the Editor on the nine, with time stopped (`Time.timeScale = 0`), a still was taken from
90 m above the start looking north-east over the volcano, with the fix's radius, then again after drawing every chunk.
SSIM was 0.99960 between the two, and the same between two stills of one state. The stills are in the PC's scratchpad
(`perf/frozen-after.png`, `perf/frozen-drawall.png`).

## Fix 2 (#178): smoke that is cheap to wake

**The nine had to load first (#183).** On dev a player's bake of the nine lost the valley's `fullDetail` from its
`map.json`, so the layout had 65-sample chunks, `PinnedGround` laid the valley's 257-sample chunks over them, and
`ValleyGround.Recover` overran in `ChemGrid.Awake`. The bake now keeps it (`GeneratedMap`, bake format 2, so old bakes are
remade), `PinnedGround.Lay` refuses a chunk of another size with a message that names the cause, and tests cover both
(`ChunkGenTests`, `PinnedGroundTests.APinnedChunkOfAnotherSizeThanTheLayoutsIsRefused`). Every number below is on top of
that fix.

**The cause was not a missing pool.** The gas effects were already pooled (`TakeFx`, `freeFx`), made with Prewarm off and
capped at 3 new ones a frame. The cost was in waking one. The Particle Pack's `SmokeEffect` prefab draws billboards, but its
`ParticleSystemRenderer` still holds the built-in Sphere mesh (515 vertices) in the mesh slot that only Mesh mode uses.
With that mesh in the slot, every wake of the renderer was slow in a player on the nine: `Instantiate` ~7 ms, the move
from the inactive staging parent to the grid ~6.7 ms, and `SetActive(true)` on a pooled one 4-10 ms. So 3 new effects were a
20-30 ms frame, and a frame that also reused pooled ones went well past that (134 ms of `RenderGas` in one frame of a
25 s diagnosis run). It was found by timing each step of `TakeFx` in a player, then cutting the prefab down in the
Editor until the cost went: the particle modules, the material, light probes and the grid's large hierarchy made no
difference, and turning the renderer off didn't either. Emptying the mesh slot did: a wake fell from 0.6-0.8 ms to 0.001-0.008 ms in an
idle Editor, and `Instantiate` to 0.12 ms. On the valley the same wake was never dear (`RenderGas` peaks at 2.7 ms there
with 73 effects made against the nine's 81), and why the nine makes it ten times dearer wasn't found.

**The fix** (`ChemGrid.Effects.cs`, `FxTemplate` / `TrimFx`): each effect prefab gets one template, a copy kept under an
inactive child of the grid and trimmed once: Prewarm off as before, and the meshes cleared from every renderer whose
mode isn't Mesh. New effects are copied from the template straight under the grid, so the staging parent and its
`SetParent` are gone. The templates for smoke, steam and sparks are made with the world (`ForgetFx`), not on the frame the
first smoke rises (that frame was 8-10 ms dearer without it). The pack's prefab isn't touched. View only (D4).
`FxTrimTests` checks that a billboard effect loses its prewarm and its mesh and that an effect drawn as meshes keeps its
own. The `fireball` perf scenario (`-perf-scenario fireball`) is new: one Fireball into the map's thickest forest, cast
as measuring starts, 6 s by default, and `fx.made` / `fx.reused` / `fx.retired` are new `PERF COUNTS`.

**Nothing drawn differently.** In the Editor on the valley, about 5 s after the scenario's Fireball, time was stopped
(`Time.timeScale = 0`) with 67 smoke effects and 599 particles alive, and three stills were taken from the scenario's
camera at 1280x720: with the fix, with the prefab's meshes put back on every live renderer (the state before the fix),
and with them cleared again. The three files are the same byte for byte. One smoke effect alone, seeded and simulated
4 s with and without the mesh, is also the same byte for byte. The stills are in the PC's scratchpad
(`fix2/moment-fix.png`, `fix2/moment-meshes-back.png`, `fix2/smoke-plain.png`, `fix2/smoke-trimmed.png`). The players'
own end-of-run stills can't show it: under `-perf-offscreen` they hold only the HUD.

## Fix 3 (#185, #186, #184): the view's cost follows the chunks drawn

The PC, 2026-11-09, `perf-fix-3` (on dev 1bb706b7). The rule is J-086: view cost follows the chunks being drawn, never
the map's size. A look change reaches the chunks drawn so far, and a chunk drawn later takes the look in force when it
draws. The nine is 729 chunks and the 12 x 10 will be 9,720, so a loop over every chunk or every cell of the map in the
view is a bug.

### Entering a chunk not drawn yet (#185)

**The cause.** Three places drew a whole chunk, or several, inside one frame:

- `DrawSome` called `DrawNear` every frame: any undrawn chunk within the streaming reach of a focus was drawn whole,
  at once (64 rows, its dressings and its blockers).
- Past that, `DrawSome` drew 8 rows at a step, and in a playtest run (`ExactFrames`, which the bot sets so a replay
  matches) two whole chunks a frame whatever they cost: 128 rows, 10 to 28 ms on the nine's walk.
- `QueueToDraw` looked at every chunk of the map each time a focus crossed into another chunk.

**The fix** (`ChemGrid.Visuals.cs`, a74ec8cd). In play nothing draws whole. `DrawNear` runs only as part of a new
world's build. After that `DrawSome` draws a row at a time, nearest a focus first, inside a budget the frame's drawing
shares: 3 ms, or in a playtest run 150 units of work, counted so a replay still matches (a row of a new chunk is 4, a
dressing or a blocker made is 1). A chunk a player lands in out of nowhere goes to the front of the queue, ahead of a
chunk half drawn. `QueueToDraw` looks only at the chunks within the draw radius of each focus. A chunk the dragon's
breath reset before it was drawn draws from its cells as they stand when its turn comes. In a playtest run a frame now
draws 150 units of work, not two whole chunks, so a replay of a play log recorded before this fix draws its chunks on
other frames than it did.

**Destroy.** The issue's "about 132 ms of `Destroy`" didn't come back: every `CoroutinesDelayedCalls` spike in these
walks is one of the bot's screenshot frames, and a 300 s walk destroys 11 dressings in all. Nothing was pooled.

### A look-changing switch and a revert (#186)

**The cause.** Three walks over the whole map, each inside one frame or spread without a budget:

- `RedrawLooks` walked the W x H `views` array and built again, at once, every dressing of a terrain whose look
  changed (300-345 ms on the nine).
- `MarkAllDrawn` walked the same array to mark every view drawn from a colour (12-46 ms).
- `RedrawEveryChunk` (a revert or a lapse) queued every chunk of the map, drawn or not, and `RenderSwept` drew six of

**The fix** (6eb40570). All three walk only the chunks drawn so far, nearest a focus first, in the same budget as

## Fix 4 (#197, #198, #199, #200): four more loops over the whole map

The PC, 2026-11-10, `perf-fix-4` (on dev 29cf1985). The same rule (J-086): what the view does each frame follows the
chunks drawn or the cells that changed, never the map's size. The nine is 729 chunks, the 12 x 10 9,720, so each of
these would have cost about 13 times as much there. One commit each, biggest first.

### The slick film's 1 s outline (#197): already gone, it was the lava's

`Contour.Film.Outline` is the marker set of a `ContourSurface` drawn whole rather than in tiles. At 2e5c6525, where the
`forestload` runs above were taken, the only such surface was the lava's (`CellLavaMesh`): one mesh round every lava and
ember cell, a box from the volcano to the Fireball's embers (1.9 M cells). The slick film (`CellSlickMesh`) was already
in 16-cell tiles (29f1512b). The Mac's #188 (4b171776, after 2e5c6525) tiled the lava, so on dev no surface is drawn
whole and the marker never fires: in `forestload` on the nine, dev and this branch alike have no `Contour.Film.Outline`
at all and `Contour.Tile.Outline` peaks at 3.2-3.3 ms. The commit only says so at the markers (f500edb2).

### Cell effects sent whole every frame (#198)

**The cause.** `CellFxTexture` (the lightning flash over charged water, an oil sheen on water) is one texel a cell, the
map's size (1728 x 1728 x 4 bytes, ~12 MB on the nine). `Advance` called `tex.Apply` on all of it in any frame a flash
was fading or one texel changed.

**The fix** (05fe6f28). A change marks its 32 x 32 block; `Advance` copies each changed block into a small staging
texture, uploads that (4 KB) and `Graphics.CopyTexture`s it into place on the GPU. Past 48 blocks in one frame (a storm
over the whole lake) the whole texture goes at once as before, and so does any platform without `CopyTexture`. The CPU
copy stays the one the shader's texels come from, so nothing drawn changes.

### Ground syncs, paint uploads and easing (#199)

**The cause.** `ValleyGround.SyncOneHeightmap` and `WriteOnePaint` each turned round a list of every chunk looking for
one with heights or paint waiting (729 looks a frame when none waited), and `CellHeightfield.Advance` looked at every
chunk's state for one still easing, and `Settled` did too.

**The fix** (4d196563). A chunk joins a queue when its heights move or its paint is composed (once, however often it
changes before its turn), and the frame takes the first in the queue; a chunk a new world has dropped is skipped.
`CellHeightfield` keeps the list of chunks with a moving rectangle, so `Advance` and `Settled` look at those alone. The
rectangles it returns are sorted as before, so the terrains get the same writes in the same order.

### Grass tinted in map order (#200)

**The cause.** A rule switch that changed the grass's colour queued every chunk of the map, in map order, and tinted one a
frame: the chunk a player stood in could wait 729 frames.

**The fix** (72671e67). The switch queues only the drawn chunks, nearest a focus first (`ChemGrid.DrawnChunksNearestFirst`);
a chunk drawn later joins the queue as it draws (`ChemGrid.ChunkDrawn`), and a chunk whose grass already wears the tint is
skipped. In the Editor with a mod turning the grass blue: the grass of the chunk the wizard stood in took it in 8 frames on
the valley and 13 on the nine, every drawn chunk in 80 (valley) and 156 (nine, where 729 were queued before), and taking
the mod off brought it back in 4-10 frames.
