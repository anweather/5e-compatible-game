# The water after a meteor shower, part 2 (2026-11-03)

Follows `2026-11-03-water.md` (#70), from two items its "Not done" left: a water tile's points, and
the ash the shower leaves to repaint. Both are the view's; the core, the tick stages, the water's
3 ms draw budget and how water and rain films are drawn are unchanged.

## What changed

- **A water tile's points read its cells once** (`CellWaterMesh.KeptCells`). Each point asked
  delegates for its top, colour and flow, and each delegate asked after the four cells round it,
  about 1100 to 1200 vertices a tile. Now a draw reads the tile's cells (and the ring round them)
  into arrays once: each cell's top, whether it's the river, and its flow (read the first time a
  point asks, as `FlowOf` was). A point blends its top and flow in one pass
  (`ContourSurface.BlendKept`) with each sum made as `BlendAround` makes it, and hands its height,
  colour and flow over at once (`ContourSurface.IPoints`). The rocks near a tile are read once a
  draw, not at every point. The slicks' films keep the delegates.
- **The ground's paint keeps each texel's noise** (`CellPaint.TexelNoise`). A texel's paint looks at
  where its point wanders, how its char mottles and how its fade slides: noise that depends on
  nothing but where the texel is. It's worked out the first time a texel paints and kept per chunk
  (about 1 MB a full-detail chunk, 64 KB a coarse one, for the chunks that paint). The cells' mixes
  are read as arrays with their spreads, not through `IReadOnlyList`, and the paint textures are
  written with less per texel.
- **The paint budget decides how many cells repaint a frame.** At most 48 a frame was the cap; with
  cheaper paint it bound well short of the 1 ms budget while the ash waited. The most is now 1000,
  a guard against a wild estimate only (PM's ruling). The budget is still 1 ms.
- For checking: `-perf-same-check` in a player draws every water tile both ways and paints every
  ground strip with its noise kept and afresh, and logs any that differ (`PERF SAME CHECK`); the
  `showerstorm` probe does the tiles for 10 s and times both ways. New markers split the paint
  (`ValleyGround.Paint.Block`, `.Paint.Compose`) and its upload (`ValleyGround.UploadPaint.Apply`).

## The same picture

- **Paint:** a golden over every texel of the strips round 300 scattered cells of a rugged world
  (walls, ash, snow, dug and scorched ground, full-detail and coarse chunks), painted twice, taken
  from the code before (`TerrainTests.PaintDrawsWhatItDidBeforeItKeptItsNoise`), fresh and kept.
  Unity's Mono keeps a sum's steps in double precision where .NET rounds each to a float (a first
  try that kept the wandered point itself changed bits under Mono only), so each runtime has its
  own golden and the kept noise is only what the noise functions return; the sums are as they were.
- **Water:** `ContourSurfaceTests` checks `BlendKept` against `BlendAround` bit for bit on random tops
  (some missing) and flows. `showerstorm` drew 285 to 385 tiles both ways in each of five runs in the
  Editor: none different.
- **In the release player** (`-perf-same-check`, after build, the whole showerstorm): 24 264 water
  tiles drawn both ways, 0 different; 334 996 paint strips painted kept and afresh, 0 different.

**The ash backlog.** Each pair of runs painted the same cells (the world runs the same ticks): 118 000
in run 1, 131 000 in runs 2 and 3, most of them rain washing ash off scorched ground.

| | Waiting when measuring starts | Drained after (est.) | Cells a painting frame | Frames painting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 7700 to 8200 | 22 to 23 s | 41 to 42 | about 40 s of them |
| after | 4900 to 5300 | 4 to 5 s | 121 to 132 | about 13 s |

"Waiting" is the peak of `ground.repaint_waiting`, 15 s after the shower ends (painting runs during
the shower too, uncounted). "Drained after" assumes it falls steadily: twice its sum over the frames
divided by the peak, in frames, at the run's mean frame. A painting frame is one that painted a cell.
So the ash drains about 3 times as fast, in the same 1 ms a frame.

## In the Editor

Hidden Editor, u2 running, `showerstorm` (Editor milliseconds are 1.5 to 2 times a player's; the
stretches differ with where the meteors land).

- **A tile's points, both ways on the same tiles:** 0.35 to 0.36 µs a vertex kept against 0.71 to
  0.74 through the delegates (three runs). In the release player: 0.357 against 0.693.
- `Contour.Tile.Points` 0.9 to 2.2 ms a frame (it still spends what the 3 ms budget leaves it: more
  tiles are drawn, 1.7 to 2.5 a frame), `Water.DrawTile` 1.1 to 2.1.
- **Paint:** the texel's cost (`Paint.Block`) is about 0.42 µs in the Editor, composing it 0.15 to
  0.2; in .NET a texel went from 762 to about 256 ns. A cell costs about 0.016 ms against 0.031 in
  part 1, and with the cap at 48 the paint spent 0.8 of its 1 ms. The upload is 0.2 to 1.1 ms a frame
  while paint is waiting, `Apply` (the textures and their mips) 0.15 to 0.55 of it.
