# Cheaper grass tufts, same reach (2026-11-23)

The Mac (M2 Pro, 16 GB), a development player (`PrototypeBuild.MacOSProfiling`), windowed 1920 x 1080, v-sync and the
frame cap off, as in `2026-11-23-nine-gpu.md`. After that day's two fixes the nine's start ran 21.2 ms a frame, and the
terrains' detail grass was the largest part left (6.9 ms). Andrew's choice: cheaper tufts and the same reach (120 m),
not a shorter reach, no grass shadows off, no fewer cascades, no render scale.

## The answer first

- **The tufts are made lighter by code** (`LightTufts`, run by the valley's build into `Generated/Ground/Tufts/`, out of
  git): every card of a pack tuft is kept, in its place with its texture, as one quad from its foot to its tip (two for
  the tall bent cards of Grass_C and the fern's fronds). Grass_A 128 -> 38 triangles, Grass_B 1,136 -> 315,
  Grass_C 1,002 -> 154, Fern_A 360 -> 74; the plants (rare) are the pack's own. The same prefabs draw the brush cells'
  clumps (`CellDressing`), which get lighter too. The materials and the shader are the pack's own: no new shader
  variants, and the meshes keep the pack's vertex layout, so the recorded PSO collections still match.
- **The nine's start: 21.3 -> 18.0 ms a frame, 46 -> 56 fps median**; the bot's walk 17.7 -> 16.6 (58 -> 68 fps median). The frame's triangles went from 8.4 M to 3.4-3.7 M (the grass's from ~6.4 M to ~1.5 M).
- **The 60 fps bar (16.7 ms) is not reached at the start within Andrew's choice.** What's left of the grass (~3 ms) is
  the shader's work per vertex (four a card now) and its shadows, not its triangles; options below, with numbers.
- **The grass already fades out, at 40-80 m, not at 120.** The pack's material (`TerrainGrass`, its `DistanceCutoff`)
  dithers a tuft away between 40 and 80 m from the camera, so the terrain draws tufts from 80 to 120 m that show no
  pixel. Drawing the grass to 80 m changes 0.86% of the ground view's pixels by more than 8 of 255 (0.01% by more than
  32) and saves 0.7 ms. The fade was kept as it is; `LightTuftsTests` checks it still ends inside the reach.

What the grass costs now (~3 ms) is the shader graph's vertex work, four vertices a card (Grass_B's 158 cards are 631
vertices however few triangles), and its shadows. Going further means fewer cards (sparser tufts) or a cheaper grass
shader than the pack's (our own, with the vertex stage cut: new shader variants and a new PSO recording).

## Then: grass to 80 m, casting no shadows (60 fps at the start)

Andrew's choice from the options above, made real rather than a knob: `MapTerrains.DetailMetres` 120 -> 80 (where the
material's fade already ends), and the terrains' detail prototypes cast no shadows (`LightTufts.For` makes a copy of
every one, the plants too with the pack's own mesh, with its shadows off). The brush cells' clumps are drawn from the
same prefabs and keep their shadows (`CellDressing.DressBrush` sets them back on): they were left as they were.
`LightTuftsTests` fails if the reach goes past the fade's end again or a prototype casts shadows; `YardAutoBuild`
rebuilds a yard whose grass still does. No shader variants change (a shadow pass fewer is drawn, none added), so the
PSO collections weren't re-recorded.
