# The water after a meteor shower (2026-11-03)

Andrew, #70: after his meteor shower his 4K game fell from about 106 fps to 50 and stayed there
through a storm and rain, bound by the main thread (main 17 to 20 ms, GPU 2 to 9). His play log
(`play-log-2026-11-02-220755.csv`, 0.1.10) shows it doesn't need the rain: at 1916 to 1956 s the sky
is clear again and main still reads 19.8 to 20.7 ms, with 60 active chunks against 36 before the
shower. What stays is the shower's aftermath and the chunks it wakes.

## What it was

Measured in a hidden Editor with the new `showerstorm` probe (Hollowmere's square, camera still: a
quiet stretch, his meteor mod for 60 s, 15 s of quiet, then a storm, read at 30, 90 and 150 s) and
the `waterlook` probe. Each stretch logs the view's markers and counts a frame (`PROBE WATERLOOK`
lines). Editor milliseconds are about 1.5 to 2 times a release player's.

1. **A water tile costs 1.4 ms, and half of that was noise.** A tile's redraw (`Water.DrawTile`) is its
   outline (`Contour.Tile.Outline`, 54%), its vertices' heights, colours and flow
   (`Contour.Tile.Points`, 46%) and the upload (`Contour.Tile.Upload`, under 0.15 ms a frame). The
   outline worked the wobble's noise out again at every lattice point a redraw, about 8 points a
   tile cell (3200 a tile, 16 hashes each), though a tile's box never moves. The points are about
   0.8 µs a vertex, 1100 to 1200 vertices a tile, in delegates per vertex: the flow cache misses
   only 0 to 10 cells a frame, so it isn't the river's currents. Nothing in it allocates per vertex.
2. **The tiles are dirty because the water really changes.** In the storm, 140 to 240 cells' water
   changes a frame: about 50 cross the drawn threshold (rain taking a cell from 1 unit to 2 and
   back, `Standing`), and the levels move 100 to 150 drawn tops a frame. Of the tiles redrawn, 15%
   came out the same as before (0.3 a frame), 8% of the moved tops moved under 2 cm, and a ground
   change with no water near dirtied 0.1 tiles a frame. So the queue (about 1000 tiles in the
   storm, 15 to 35 after the shower) never empties, and `WaterMesh` spends its whole 3 ms budget
   every frame: what it costs a frame is the budget, not the work.
3. **The water levels ran back to back.** Any level change sets the levels stale, and in a storm or
   by a fire one changes every frame. A pass over 2270 wet cells takes about 5 frames at its 2 ms
   budget (11 ms, 5 µs a wet cell in the Editor), and the next began as soon as it ended: 2.3 ms a
   frame, every frame.
4. **`ValleyGround.Flush` was repainting the shower's ash.** The fires leave 25 000 cells waiting to
   repaint after the shower, 32 a frame within the paint's 1 ms budget, plus 0.7 to 1 ms a frame
   uploading one chunk's paint textures (`UploadPaint`). In the storm, 40 of the 45 cells painted a
   frame painted just what they had (the rain washing ash off ground that's scorched anyway, so
   charred either way). `SyncHeightmap` (a new marker) stays under 0.1 ms.
5. **With 58 active chunks** the release player's 15.5 ms frame is the tick 1.8 ms, the view
   (`ChemGrid.Render`) 9.2 and the rest of Unity's frame about 4.5. In the Editor's storm: the tick
   2.7 to 3.2 ms (`flow` about 1 ms a frame, `watercheck` 0.5 to 0.6, `timers` 0.4, `rest` 0.3 to
   0.4), the view 9 (the water 4.5, the slicks' mud films 1.4 to 2.0, the walk over the active
   chunks' cells 2.5 to 2.9), `ValleyFauna` 2.0 to 2.3, `Flush` 0.3 to 1.4. What grows with the
   chunks is the cell walk (1.4 ms at 36 chunks, 2.6 to 2.9 at 50 to 59, about 45 ns a cell, a
   third of the active cells a frame) and the tick (`watercheck` 0.16 ms quiet, 0.5 to 0.6 after).
   The rain's own cost is the tick's `flow` and the wet-and-dry flicker that keeps the water and
   slick tiles redrawing.

## What changed

- **A tile keeps its noise** (`CellContour`, tiled only): worked out the first time a lattice point is
  inside, kept until the box moves. The film keeps working it out each build. Noise points worked
  out in the storm fell from 3800 to 4800 a frame to 20 to 220.
- **The levels rest 8 frames between passes** (`CellWaterMesh.RestFrames`): a level can be drawn
  that much later than it moved.
- **Ground paint skips a cell whose mix is the one it's already painted with**, without spending the
  frame's budget, up to 2000 looked at a frame (`ValleyGround.TakeBatch`). A neighbour composed
  early with a mix it isn't painted with is marked to paint in full on its turn.
- `showerstorm`, a probe and a perf scenario (`-perf-scenario showerstorm`; `-perf-no-rain` keeps
  the sky clear after the shower); `-perf-offscreen-4k`; counts of why tiles are dirtied
  (`water.mark.*`), redraws that changed nothing (`tile.unchanged`), noise points, repaints that
  were the same (`ground.repaint_same`); a `ValleyGround.SyncHeightmap` marker.

## Not done

- **A tile's points**: about 0.8 µs a vertex in delegates; blending from per-tile arrays of tops and
  flows would likely halve a tile again.
- **The draw budget**: in a storm the queue never empties, so `WaterMesh` always spends its 3 ms.
  Tiles are now cheaper, so 2 ms would draw about as many as 3 did before. Kept at 3: that's a call
  on water freshness against milliseconds.
- **The wet-and-dry flicker** at 1 and 2 units (`Standing`): about 50 changes a frame in rain.
  Holding a drawn cell until it's dry would stop it, but rain's film would then show as specks.
- **The cell walk** grows with the chunks (45 ns a cell). A list of changed cells from the core would
  replace it: that's the core's, so it's a proposal.
- **The ash backlog** after a shower: 25 000 cells at 32 a frame took about two minutes to drain in
  the probe (13 500 left at 30 s of storm, 1700 at 90 s), 1 ms of paint and up to 1 ms of upload a
  frame all the while.
- **`tick.flow`** about 1 ms a frame in the storm: a tick stage, for the PM.
- Not measured: a real window, GPU time, hosting or three players (his 58 chunks were his alone),
  repeats of either run, the Mac. Not looked at on film: levels drawn 8 frames late, the paint skip.
