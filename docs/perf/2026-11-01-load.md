# The lag under load (2026-11-01)

Andrew: "yeet a fireball at the forest, then start doing other things". His session of
2026-11-01 (0.1.10, `play-log-2026-11-01-014225.csv`, 31 minutes at 3840x2160, hosting three
players) averaged 69 fps with a 1% low of 7.5, 747 hitches and two F8 marks in its last two
minutes.

## What it was

Not chemistry. Ticks cost at most 6 ms a frame throughout; the world only fell behind (sim 37 to
49%) because the frame rate starved that budget.

1. **Rain's water.** Rain takes the wet cells to 34 000, and the water's levels and banks ran over
   all of them nearly every frame, 20 to 30 ms. Tiles of 32 cells took 10 to 15 ms each, so the
   3 ms budget could only draw one and overrun. The slicks' single film took hundreds of ms a
   redraw on dev. That climb is what his F8 marks are about.
2. **The host's desync hash.** 115 to 135 ms in one frame, every 5.0 s, however quiet the game:
   a string of every cell in the world, 26 MB of garbage a call. Online only.
3. **A flat 4 ms a frame**, walking 150 000 cells to find what changed, in every scene.
4. **Dead whirl columns.** `ResetWorld` destroyed them and `columns` kept them: 37 646
   NullReferenceExceptions. No measurable frame cost (10.42 ms against 10.50, 2457 exceptions in
   1916 frames), but each one skipped the rest of the frame's view work.

GPU time rose from 4 to 45 ms in the before runs and stays near 7 after, with no change to what is
drawn. Hiding the water, the slicks or every particle system made no difference to it, so none of
those is why. It follows the main thread's work; what ties them isn't established.

4K costs little on this PC: GPU p50 3.8 ms at 3840x2160 against 4.5 at 1920x1080 at the same load
before the rain.

Two joined peers cost the host about 3 ms of main (6.9 to 9.9), with all three copies on this one
PC, which is most of the gap between his 13 ms and these runs' 9.

## What changed

- **Water levels** run resumably, 2 ms a frame (`CellWaterMesh.Recompute`). A level can be a moment late.
- **Water and slick tiles** are 16 cells, with no delegates made per vertex; flows and banks are flat
  arrays; a tile only looks at the rocks near it; a slick tile caches each cell's colour and grade.
  A slick's steepness fade is now blended from cell middles rather than worked out at every vertex.
- **A frame a tick has spent** (3 ms or more) draws no tiles.
- **The active chunks** are looked over a third a frame (`ChemGrid.Render`): a changed cell can be
  drawn two frames later.
- **The hash** is `RollingHash`: a slice of the cells a tick, picked by the tick number and the world's
  size alone, closed every 30 ticks. `CellHash` builds no string. A desync is now compared whichever
  side's hash arrives second; before, a client already past the tick never compared it.
- **`ForgetFx`** forgets the whirl columns.
